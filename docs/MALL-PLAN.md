# The dead mall — plan (ideas to curate down)

Status: ideas only. Nothing here is built. Far more is listed than we will
use; the point is to cross things out. My own picks are marked **[pick]**.
Theme id (working): `mall`. Branch: `claude/suburb-floor` until told otherwise.

## The premise, in three steps

1. **Office.** It is still the same building. Ceiling tiles, fluorescent tubes,
   carpet tile, fire doors, a facilities department that signed all this off.
2. **Mall.** Somebody on this floor built a shopping centre indoors: a food
   court, a concourse, shopfronts, an escalator, a car park. At closing time.
3. **Wrong.** Whoever built it had only ever had a mall *described* to them,
   by somebody who had it described to *them*. They know there are shops, that
   things cost money, that you go up and down, that a voice tells you things,
   and that you park. They do not know why.

The suburb floor is daylight that never ends. The mall is **closing time that
never finishes**: half the lights are already off, shutters are half down, the
floor is wet, and the voice keeps saying the centre closes in five minutes.

What makes it ours and not "liminal mall" as everybody has seen it:
- It is plainly built *by an office*: shop signs are laminated A4, the food
  court is a tea point scaled up, price tags are expense codes.
- The misunderstanding is consistent: **they think the shopping is the
  product.** Shops sell bags. Bags contain receipts. Receipts are for bags.
- The building's own glitch language carries over from the suburb's Gap:
  things not drawn, squares without their texture, a thing drawn twice.

## Look and materials

- **Floor:** terrazzo in big squares (cheap: one texture), with a brass strip
  every few metres. Wet-floor sheen in patches. Carpet tile showing through
  where the terrazzo "runs out". **[pick]**
- **Walls:** shopfront glazing that is a painted flat; roller shutters; tiled
  columns; a mirrored column that reflects a different room.
- **Ceiling:** the office grid, but with one coffered "atrium" per big room:
  a hole in the tiles painted as a skylight, night outside, a painted moon
  with the wrong number of craters. Sometimes the atrium is real and four
  storeys of balcony go up into dark. **[pick]**
- **Light:** dim by default (the suburb is bright; this is its opposite).
  Shop signs are the light: lit boxes in sodium, pink and hospital green.
  One sign per room flickers in a pattern. **[pick]**
- **Sound** (voices we already have, no files unless you add them): the hum,
  a tannoy chime, shutters, an escalator's click. Muzak would need a file —
  yours to decide; the level works without it.
- **Signage:** garbled like the suburb's shops, but *retail* garble: SALE
  ENDS, 3 FOR 2 FOR 3, EVERYTHING MUST, LAST FEW DAYS (since 1987).
  Wayfinding totems where every arrow points at YOU ARE HERE. **[pick]**
- **Wall paint variants** (like sky / corn / hedge on the suburb): shopfront
  run · car-park concrete with bay numbers · service corridor breeze-block ·
  tiled toilet green · hoarding ("EXCITING NEW STORE COMING") .

## Plain-room vocabulary (the maze between set pieces)

The mall must keep the tight, confusing connective tissue. Candidates:
- **Service corridors**: narrow, breeze-block, pipes, a stencilled number
  that goes up on the way in and also up on the way out. **[pick]**
- **Shutter rows**: a room lined with shutters at different heights; some can
  be crawled under, most are a painted flat. **[pick]**
- **Kiosk islands**: a room with one to three small booths as pillars
  (phone cases, keys cut, "photos of you" — see set pieces).
- **Planter benches**: fake plants in terrazzo boxes as low cover.
- **Changing-room runs**: a corridor of curtained cubicles, too many, some
  curtains swaying. Natural crawl / peek spaces.
- **Stock rooms**: floor-to-ceiling brown boxes with one shop's name, aisles
  one body wide (the walk-in closet's maze, in cardboard).
- **Mezzanine edges**: a room split by a balcony rail with a drop; stairs
  up to a door at the top of the wall.
- **Lawn oddity equivalents** (the 40% "one thing wrong" in bare rooms):
  a pile of left shoes · a trolley train going into the wall · a fountain
  with no water but the coins are there · a mannequin family at a table ·
  a bench facing the wall · a kiddie ride facing the wall, running ·
  a "wet floor" sign forest · a shop shutter in the floor · a lit sign for a
  shop that is 40 cm deep · an escalator handrail with no escalator ·
  a queue rope maze around nothing · a photo booth with boots under the
  curtain and nobody in it · a bin that is full of bins.

## Headline rooms (set pieces) — many, so no two visits match

1. **The Food Court.** Bolted-down tables in a grid, eight counters, each
   serving one thing misunderstood (a tray of trays; soup of the day:
   "DAY"; a carvery of a single enormous sandwich). Trays slide on the
   tables by themselves toward whoever sat down last. One counter serves a
   real snack. **[pick]**
2. **The Escalators.** A pair, both going up. The top is the bottom. Walk
   against one and you get somewhere; ride it and you arrive where you
   started, a little later in the evening (lights one step dimmer each lap).
   Real mechanic: only *walking against* an escalator makes progress. **[pick]**
3. **The Atrium.** Four storeys of balcony around a void, a dead fountain at
   the bottom. Uses the Gap's rule: the void kills. The lift is a cupboard.
   The way across is the banner hanging from the far balcony (a loose flat
   again — the bridge system) or the long way round every storey.
4. **The Car Park.** Low concrete, bay numbers that repeat, your car is every
   car. A ticket machine that wants the ticket you were given on a
   different floor. A barrier arm that is the only moving thing. Ramps that
   go down to the same level. (Differs from the suburb's car park: that one
   is a lawn with cars sinking; this is the real concrete kind, endless.)
   **[pick]**
5. **The Department Store.** Mannequins. Covered under entities below — the
   room is its home: racks as cover, fitting rooms, a perfume counter that
   fogs the camera.
6. **The Arcade.** Cabinets all playing a game of *this corridor*, from
   behind you, live. One cabinet shows where something else is.
7. **The Anchor Store That Never Opened.** A hoarding: OPENING SOON. Behind
   it, the shell: bare slab, columns, cables, and the Gap's un-drawn
   squares where the shop was never made. The biggest dark room we have.
8. **The Toy Shop.** Shelves of one toy: a model of the mall. One model has
   a tiny you in it. Moving the tiny furniture moves the real furniture
   (ties into the movable-props system; expensive — parked).
9. **The Cinema.** One screen, showing the car park, live. Seats full of
   coats. An usher's torch moving along the rows with nobody holding it.
10. **The Fountain Court.** Coins on the dry bottom. Taking one costs you
    something later (the tannoy knows). Throwing one in *buys* something: a
    light back on, a shutter up.
11. **Lost Property.** A counter and shelves of everything players have
    dropped or lost on any floor above (we know the inventory). Yours is
    there. Claiming it needs the right slip.
12. **The Pet Shop.** Tanks, cages, all with the price and no animal. One
    tank has water moving. (Mood room; cheap.)
13. **Santa's Grotto, in the wrong month.** A queue rope maze into a plywood
    grotto. The chair is occupied by a sack. Photos were taken: they are on
    the wall, and you are in the last one.
14. **The Toilets.** A tiled corridor of cubicles that turns left four times
    and is longer each time. Hand dryers start as you pass.
15. **Customer Services.** A desk, a ticket dispenser, a number display. Your
    number is called on another floor. Take a ticket here → something
    elsewhere in the building is "resolved" (a door, a shutter).
16. **The Photo Booth Row.** Each booth prints a strip of where you will be
    in four rooms' time. Sometimes there is somebody else in frame four.
17. **Bag Shop / Receipt Shop.** The misunderstanding made a room: shelves of
    carrier bags; each holds a receipt for a bag.
18. **The Loading Dock.** Roller doors, a pallet truck, shrink-wrapped
    pallets of fake plants. A door to "outside" that is a painted car park at
    night on a flat — which a later room is really behind.
19. **The Travelator.** A long flat moving walkway in a tube of adverts for
    this mall. Standing still moves you; walking keeps you level with the
    adverts, which then change to be about you.
20. **Key-cutting Kiosk.** Cuts a key of whatever you hold up to it. A key cut
    from a snack opens the snack machine. (Item-combination puzzle; parked.)

## What you *do* here that is new (gameplay)

The suburb's new verbs were: lean on a prop, carry and lay a bridge, be
followed. The mall should add its own, not repeat those. Candidates:

- **Closing time as a clock. [pick]** The tannoy counts down a closing that
  never comes, but each announcement turns something off: a bank of lights,
  an escalator, a shutter comes down across a doorway (a real, slow,
  duck-under-it shutter). The floor gets darker and narrower the longer you
  stay. Not a fail timer: a pressure. Throwing a coin in the fountain, or
  the customer-services ticket, buys some of it back.
- **Shutters you can duck. [pick]** A shutter coming down is passable until
  it is below crouch height. Some shut on things that are following you.
- **Go against the flow. [pick]** Escalators and the travelator: the mall
  moves you where it wants; progress is walking against it.
- **Be a customer.** Holding a carrier bag makes you *part of the mall*: the
  mannequins ignore you, the tannoy stops naming you. But a bag fills by
  itself and slows you; put it down and they notice. **[pick]**
- **Spend.** Coins (we already have coins) become meaningful on this one
  floor: vending, fountain, photo booth, kiddie ride (a ride you pay for
  that takes you through a wall).
- **Trolley.** A movable (Codex's system) you can *ride*: push, hop on, roll
  down a car-park ramp past something. Or a trolley train as a battering
  ram / barricade.
- **Read the map.** Wayfinding totems are wrong in a consistent way (always
  rotated a quarter turn, or showing the floor above). Learnable.
- **The tannoy lies about you, then stops lying.** Flavour early
  ("Would the owner of a grey camcorder…"); information later ("…is
  reminded that the mascot is on Level 2"). Uses what the game knows.

## Who lives here (entities)

Rule from the suburb: **a new creature only for a new way of playing;
otherwise reuse the cast.** Existing cast roams here as everywhere.

New, if we want one headline creature — pick ONE:
- **The Mannequins (the Fitting). [pick]** A family: fibreglass, jointed
  wrong. Not "moves when unwatched" (the mascot owns that). Theirs: **they
  are dressed as the last thing you did.** They copy your *route*: every
  time you re-enter a room, one more of them is standing where you stood,
  in your pose, facing your way out. Rooms you cross a lot fill up. They
  only hurt when a room is full enough that you have to push between two.
  Pressure on backtracking; the counter is to not go the same way twice.
- **Security.** A torch beam and a radio, no body. It patrols a fixed loop
  you can learn; in the beam you are "escorted" back to Customer Services
  (set back, not hurt). Fair, readable, very mall.
- **The Tannoy** as an entity: no body at all. It announces where you are
  to everything else on the floor unless you are "a customer" (bag) or
  standing under a speaker that has been unplugged.
- **The Cleaner's machine.** A ride-on floor polisher going round by itself
  leaving wet floor behind it; wet floor is slippery (slide in the direction
  you were going). Environmental, not a hunter. Could be a room hazard.
- **Santa (the sack).** Lives in the grotto; gives you something you lost.
  Helps. Costs a photo.
- **The Shoppers.** Coats on a rail that moves along the ceiling track
  through several rooms, like a dry-cleaner's conveyor, at walking pace.
  You can hide among them. Something else also does.
- **The Sale.** A crowd-shaped absence: rope barriers that rearrange into a
  queue around you if you stand still. Soft trap.

Reuse ideas for the existing cast:
- Toner in the car park ceiling voids (low concrete suits it).
- Shadow in shop windows (a reflection that is not you).
- Moth and the lit shop signs (it goes to the next light: there are many).
- Mascot on the kiddie ride.

## Items

One mall-only pickup (as the garage opener is to the suburb). Candidates:
- **Carrier bag [pick]** — "be a customer" above.
- **Loyalty card** — opens staff doors; each use takes a point; points are
  hearts.
- **Parking ticket** — validated at Customer Services; lifts the car-park
  barrier, which is a shortcut to the way down.
- **Receipt** — a strip of paper listing rooms you have *not* been in yet.
- **Coin tray** — coins matter here (see Spend).
- **Sample** — perfume: sprayed, the camera fogs pink and creatures lose
  you for a few seconds.

## Weird, specifically (the "telephone" list)

Things only somebody who had a mall described to them would build:
- Shops that sell the *idea* of the shop: a shoe shop of one left shoe in
  every size; a bookshop of one book, the mall's own fire evacuation plan.
- Prices that are not money: £ "soon", 3 for a Tuesday, FREE (deposit).
- An escalator of **carpet**. A lift with buttons for B, G, G, G, 1.
- A food court where the food is on the *menu boards* and the boards are
  on the tables.
- Benches bolted to the ceiling above the benches on the floor, in case.
- A fountain that is a photo of a fountain, wet.
- Mannequins in the windows *shopping* — looking in at the empty mall.
- Christmas and a summer sale and a back-to-school at once, each a third
  of the decorations.
- A "You are here" sticker on you (seen in every mirror).
- Fire exits signed in every direction including up.
- The un-drawn: a shop unit that is only its floor plan, chalked. A stair
  whose risers have no treads. A sign with the lorem ipsum still in.
- One shop is completely normal, fully stocked, lit, and open, and you
  cannot find its door.

## Suggested first slice (what I would build first)

1. Theme `mall`, `dealt: false`: terrazzo, dim, lit shop signs, service
   corridors and shutter rows as the plain vocabulary, six lawn-oddity
   equivalents.
2. Three headline rooms that need no shared changes: **Food Court**,
   **Car Park**, **Department Store**.
3. **The Mannequins** as the floor's creature (route-copying), living in the
   department store and spreading.
4. Then ask for the two shared pieces that give the floor its own verbs:
   **ducking shutters** and **moving floors** — and build the Escalators and
   the closing-time clock on them.
5. Item: the **carrier bag**, once the inventory line is agreed.
