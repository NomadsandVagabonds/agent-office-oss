# BlockRooms · Yellow Office

Ten hallway pieces and four feature rooms for the opening floor. The art uses the game's Status Codes and Hydration Corridor screenshots as visual references. The routes below are proposed layouts for a first playable pass.

**Selected for the build · The Door on the Chain.** The player should be able to line up the brass links, the ordinary door beyond and the gap below in a single glance. Prototype the approach from both directions. [See the chain concept](door-on-the-chain.png).

## Beyond Quarter Turn

### Inside Lane
A yellow office corridor makes a banked U-turn. Take the low inside line around the divider and come back alongside the entrance half a storey higher. On the outside of the turn, the carpet climbs almost to a doorknob. Block out the inner path at a walkable incline first. Let the outer edge climb steeply enough to nearly swallow the door. [Full-size concept](inside-lane.png)

### Shear Line
Three strips of what used to be one hallway slide past each other at different heights. Enter on a high side strip. Descend the first diagonal patch to the low central doorway or cross to the farther ramp and climb to a different office on the opposite side. A drinking fountain left at the old floor line gives the eye a fixed height. Give each crossing a clear landing wide enough for two players to pass. [Full-size concept](shear-line.png)

### Low Point
The skirting board runs straight across the hall like a ruler. The carpet dips so far below it that you can walk downhill, duck under the wall and climb back into the next office. A pencil rests where everything dropped on this floor would roll. At the entrance, let the player see a little lit carpet on the far side. At the bottom, let the wall hide everything except the way through. [Full-size concept](low-point.png)

### The Underside
Descend a switchback around a narrow shaft. At the midpoint, the underside of the hall you just crossed turns out to have its own carpet, wood trim and upside-down office door. A second, upright doorway leads out below it. A fluorescent in the walking surface lights the wrong door from beneath. Build the walk first, then sight the inverted threshold from the turn where both doors come into view. [Full-size concept](the-underside.png)

## Hallways and connectors

### Operable Wall
**Two ends, one blind turn.** Enter through the open end of a folding meeting-room partition. A brass finger pull sits at shoulder height, then another, then twelve. At the tightest fold you can see the room you just left through a slit beside your face. Follow the ceiling trolley track around the bend to the next office. Build one repeatable panel, hinge and track, with a fixed collision strip along each side. Make this the common connector. [Full-size concept](operable-wall.png)

### Right of Way
**Two ends, with one stretch of path visible twice.** Cross the carpeted bridge and look down at a water cooler in the lower hallway. A shallow ramp behind the far wall doubles back underneath. At the cooler, look up and recognize the loose rail post you just passed. Put the exit beyond the cooler so the revelation lands on the way forward. The walk can be continuous in ordinary 3D space. [Full-size concept](right-of-way.png)

### Triplicate
**One entrance, two onward exits, one loop.** Three parallel aisles appear to end at the same blue wastebasket. Long eye-level slots through the staggered dividers let you keep sight of it as you walk. The middle aisle leads straight through, the left one peels into a neighboring room, and the right one folds back to the approach. Use a single physical bin. Test the sightline from all three mouths before dressing the hall. [Full-size concept](triplicate.png)

### Quarter Turn
**Two ends, a level walk.** From the first fluorescent bay everything looks square. Over the next six bays the room shell turns 15 degrees at a time. Eventually a door lies sideways, a lit ceiling panel is beside your elbow, and the carpet underfoot still feels completely ordinary. Leave the collision floor and camera level. Put a square door beyond the twist so the player can watch it stay upright. Save this one for a rare straightaway. [Full-size concept](quarter-turn.png)

### Pedestrian Crossing
**A T-junction with a working crossing button.** A zebra crossing continues straight up the wallpaper, across the acoustic tiles and back down. Push an amber button and hear a relay on the far side. After a beat, the fluorescent in one branch comes on and lights the turn. Both branches are walkable throughout. Start with stepped strips of chalky paint aligned across all four surfaces and one practical light. [Full-size concept](pedestrian-crossing.png)

### The Door on the Chain
**Two ends, with a short low passage.** What looks like a wood-paneled hall is the gap behind one enormous half-open door. The closer you get to the brass chain, the smaller the normal office door beyond it looks. Walk beneath the raised end to reach that door. Use the existing automatic duck where needed. Check clearance from the return direction and make the hanging middle read clearly from player eye height. [Full-size concept](door-on-the-chain.png)

## Feature rooms from the first round

### Carpet Allowance
A wall-to-wall carpet was cut several meters too long. The excess rises in a single great pleat, with exposed backing and an opening tall enough to duck into. Take the short blind route under the fold or follow its edge around and see where the carpet finally fits. A normal stapler at the entrance sells the scale. [Full-size concept](carpet-allowance.png)

### Borrowed Daylight
A wheeled Venetian blind stands by itself in a windowless room. A paper cup interrupts the slat shadows on the carpet. The light picks out the jamb of a recessed hall. Walk past the blind and around the jamb to leave. A baked light pattern crossing the floor and opposite wall is sufficient for the first pass. [Full-size concept](borrowed-daylight.png)

### Interoffice Mail
A normal desktop letter tray sits in front of three room-sized smoked-amber trays. Each tray holds its own strip-lit office and ordinary brown door. Enter on the middle tray, round the back of its short sidewall, and descend to the lower tray to leave. Keep a clear sightline back to the miniature on the desk. Prototype two playable trays and dress the third as a visible upper office. [Full-size concept](interoffice-mail.png)

### Please Be Seated
The chair at the end of a long straight hall looks normal until you reach its front legs. Walk between them and turn back. The legs frame an ordinary door, the seat back pushes into the fluorescent grid, and the distance you just walked finally makes sense. Use one fixed oversized chair and tune the approach sightline at standing eye height. [Full-size concept](please-be-seated.png)

## Suggested first playable pair

Build **The Door on the Chain** first. Try **Low Point** as the next graybox. Place one between two existing offices, then walk it in both directions from the normal first-person camera. From the top of the trough, can you see the hint of the next office? From underneath, does the straight skirting board feel like the bottom of a wall? **Operable Wall** is a second option for a common bend, and **Triplicate** supplies a junction with two useful ways forward.
