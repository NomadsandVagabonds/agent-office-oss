# ImageGen prompts

The seven main plates used the shared direction below, followed by each scene description. The look-back used Passing Place as its continuity reference.

Make one wide 16:9 in-game environment concept frame for BlockRooms, a first-person browser voxel horror game. First reference is the actual Yellow Office kit; preserve its faded diamond wallpaper, olive felt carpet, walnut baseboards, low square acoustic panels and practical fluorescent strips. Second reference shows the suffocating proportions of a narrowing walkway and a distant chair. Third shows the density of recognizable, slightly wrong objects intersecting architecture. Fourth anchors material finish and believable but impossible slopes. A detailed, deliberately faceted mid-1990s office: chunky modeled edges, worn plastic, visible fasteners, matte vinyl, tile seams, contact shadows at intersections. Avoid a photorealistic modern abandoned building or bare playtest cubes. Human-height walkable camera, one coherent view, no HUD, titles, text, collage or branding. Yellow, nicotine cream, olive, wood, and a small object-color accent. Keep grounded, quiet and uncanny.

## Passing Place

A huge low continuous square office floor tapers into a truly frightening slit just wide enough for ONE desk chair. Show the transition as the subject. From about six metres back, a sharp tapering diagonal sidewall takes up more and more of the room until it runs parallel to the other wall, making a LONG very narrow hallway still visible beyond the throat. It is a funnel in plan, not just a door cut into a distant wall. In the pinch put exactly one loose burgundy fabric 5-caster task chair, turned three quarters and blocking the entire lane, upright and visibly resting on the carpet, its back framed by a far fluorescent. On viewer RIGHT AT the chair there is a little square coat-alcove big enough to push the chair sideways into; the forward slot continues past it towards a weak greenish pool of light. In the wide foreground an abandoned normal beige metal filing cabinet is half fused sideways into the left wall: handle, label holder, drawer lip visible, clean impossible intersection, no rubble. Use repeated luminous ceiling tiles, a strip of dark skirting and visible squares of carpet to measure extreme compression. The chair and nook must be readable in the hero frame. Empty room. No human or monster.

## Continuous Feed

From a waist-high brass railing at the MIDDLE landing of a four-storey windowless claims department, see a monumental concertina of continuous-feed computer paper descending through the entire atrium. Broad cream sheets with faint green accounting rules, punched tractor holes along BOTH edges, dotted perforations marking fold crests. Crisp folded paper almost stiff as floors. A full-size normal wheeled chair and fire door on each balcony establish the extraordinary scale. The paper accordion forms broad playable zigzag ramps from the yawning open void to an 85cm-wide maintenance slot behind a gigantic midnight-blue tractor roller near the bottom; the player can see where the paper slips into the yellow wall. Put one torn paper corner caught around a ceiling fixture and a realistically modeled manila file sticking halfway out of a balcony wall. Layer three landings against an asymmetrical receding wall. The silhouette of the paper path and the black rows of sprocket holes must be instantly legible. No conventional grand staircase. A distant strip of weak light at the exit.

## False Summit

A windowless expanse of continuous olive office carpet folded into three broad, towering, asymmetric geometric ridges beneath one absolutely level suspended ceiling. Like a whole section of the office buckled upward and the facilities contractor simply carpeted it. The broad first valley can hold a whole department; by the far crest the walkable path squeezes into a shoulder-width slot BETWEEN the carpeted ridge and wallpapered wall, underneath an uncomfortably low row of strip lights. Frame from halfway up the first crest, see across two crests; a high closed plumb wooden office door half engulfed by the ridgeline, and on the left foreground a PAIR of squared chrome visitor-chair arms and top third of a pinstriped seat back surface out of solid carpet, contact shadows and fibers meeting the metal. Not sand: visible patterned carpet tiles, crisp angular floor breaks, straight baseboards disappearing into crests. Lines of normal glass office bays in the far wall at progressively absurd floor heights sell scale. No atrium rails, no endless staircase. One normal potted plant at elbow height near the exit slit.

## Joining Desks

A small cramped clerical room bisected by a floor-to-ceiling solid wallpapered partition laid DIRECTLY THROUGH the middle of a 1993 dental-green Formica desk. Composition three-quarter close to the END of the partition so in a SINGLE image you see the tabletop, drawer pedestal and cable bundle go into plaster on one side and the matching grain, half of a chunky beige CRT and the SAME red telephone cord protrude from the other face. The partition has real thickness and a perfectly intact brown skirting strip. The solids interpenetrate with sharp contact shadows, nothing broken, no cutaway diagram, no gaping holes. At the foreground skirt a dropped sheaf of pale pink carbon paper goes partway into the floor, sheets slightly curled, holes in first sheet. Asset craft is the draw: a pen tray with 2 pens, bevel of CRT housing and vent ribs, screwed drawer pulls, a coiled phone cord and one pale circle left by a coffee cup. From the near stationary desk a narrow path leads around the partition to a lit normal-size door. A quiet uncanny room, asymmetric and closely observed.

## Long Distance

An awkwardly narrow pair of blind right-angle turns in a low light yellow office hall, foreground wider, far pinch shoulder width. A single normal avocado-green office telephone handset hangs off-hook at ear height against the immediate left wall over a little desk shelf sunk into plaster. Its dense dark green spiral cord emerges from ONE side of the receiver and goes cleanly INSIDE the wall. Through an offset doorless cutout see the SAME curly cord emerge on the other side, continue horizontally along the baseboard and slip under a closed wooden door at the last corner. Use the pale cord shadow and occasional staples as a graphic player-leading line. No giant phone. Old keypad with inset yellowed numerals, worn holes in earpiece, short indexing label, tarnished screws. The main route turns right at the cord, reinforced by the brass footplate worn bright. The last 2m feel barely body wide. Fluorescent afterglow recedes into the elbow; no character. One oblique intimate perspective.

## The Coat Check

A modest fluorescent employee coat closet viewed from its open doorway. A rail of only FIVE blocky old winter coats, nubby charcoal wool, olive nylon, one sand trench. Each garment has individually legible voxel lapels, seams, toggles, a hanging-loop and bent hangers. The hem of the dark coat is cleanly sunk halfway into the floor and one sleeve intersects solid wallpaper like this is routine. CENTRAL image: one unbuttoned tan coat, held open and partly worked into the wall, reveals THE ACTUAL NARROW YELLOW HALLWAY INSIDE ITS LINING. The lapels and collar precisely frame a perspective of baseboards, carpet, three shrinking strip lights and a tiny door several metres deep. A player's path from a nearly empty changing room under that rail is a short stoop and then a walk through the coat. Favor a mundane material overlap over any glowing portal or decorative sci-fi effects. A little red plastic coat-check token fallen under the rail for scale. Keep one central anomaly, rich physical details.

## Passing Place · look-back

Create the companion reverse-angle gameplay concept to the supplied frame. Same BlockRooms Yellow Office set, same low bay of fluorescents, tan diamond wallpaper, walnut skirting with one bright nail head, olive carpet squares, SAME modest burgundy armless five-caster fabric task chair and the exact little nook with coat hooks. One cinematic wide 16:9 environment render. Time has advanced two seconds. Previously the chair blocked the narrow lane. Now the player has dragged it backwards out of the lane and put it UNDER THE COAT HOOKS inside the little side recess. Position the viewpoint just INSIDE the almost impossibly narrow passage the chair used to block, facing BACK OUT into the broad office. Near edges left and right are VERY close wallpaper walls barely 0.8m apart, filling 40 percent of the frame. A few inches ahead is the dramatic release into a very broad low-ceilinged yellow office with several banks of fluorescents. We should feel our shoulders could touch the near walls. Looking in reverse means the recess and its burgundy chair are on CAMERA LEFT just past the narrowest point, the chair seat is clearly confined to the nook, and every inch of the center floor is now clear. The face of the old filing cabinet intersects the wall to camera right further out. Carpet caster tracks lead around the lip into the recess. In the far wide room throw a single tall crooked HUMANLIKE shadow across the floor from an unseen pursuer rounding a corner; only a hint, recognizably head and shoulder. Ordinary utilitarian high-detail voxel game asset finish. Do not add people, extra chairs, or a HUD. Prioritize sense of tight foreground and clear way through before anything else.
