## Design Context

### Users
People coordinating agents in The Office, taking a short break via the elevator. The owner wants cultural office spoofs and small games: recognizable places to explore without leaving the building's voxel world or disturbing ongoing work.

### Brand Personality
Affectionate, mundane, absurd. Reward exploration, small observations, and harmless office mischief. Each floor may have its own mood, from sitcom warmth to quiet workplace unease, while keeping the humor grounded in ordinary office objects.

### Aesthetic Direction
Use the house's chunky voxel scale and actual Vox geometry across distinct cultural break floors. Instructions and feedback live on physical memos, signs, and equipment, per the level kit. Original models, layouts, and workplace jokes establish each homage without borrowed game or television assets.

- **Scranton Paper Co.**: a small Pennsylvania paper-company branch with beige carpet, wood veneer, venetian blinds, fluorescent ceiling panels, reception, sales islands, a manager's office, a conference room, and a kitchenette. First-person exploration and an optional timed prank.
- **Night Shift**: a monochrome office stealth game inspired by the atmosphere of Limbo. A side-on orthographic camera, black voxel silhouettes, distant windows, dust, and sweeping white security beams replace the forest setting. Light exposes the player; low desks and darkness provide cover. Keep the scene legible enough to learn by watching.
- **After Hours**: another floor of the same agent office, now infested. Reuse the real office's wood panelling, warm carpet, CRT desks, chairs, bookshelves, plants, and kitchenette; keep connected rooms and server aisles. Avoid a warehouse or military-facility atmosphere. No instructional signs, room-label plaques, floor arrows, or textual mission screens: the owner finds these immersion-breaking. Use physical equipment, amber/green indicators, and a compact wrist display with health and equipment icons. Headcrabs prepare their leaps, recoil on the first crowbar hit, and die on the second.

### Design Principles
1. This is a break from coordination; local play never changes real work.
2. Make the room recognizable through layout, props, and original workplace jokes.
3. Keep stakes forgiving: Scranton's timer is optional; Night Shift has unlimited checkpoint retries and a pause key.
4. Show consequences in the room: a stapler disappears, gelatin wobbles, a beam stops at cover, a shutter opens when power is cut.
5. Keep the shared elevator and Escape return-to-work control. Adapt movement to each game; After Hours deliberately omits instructional signs.
6. Pause active game time when the visitor is interrupted. Respect reduced-motion settings without removing necessary gameplay signals.
